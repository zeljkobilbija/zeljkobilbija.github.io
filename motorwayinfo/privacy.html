Motorway Info – Privacy Policy

Effective date: 21 November 2026 (updated: traffic news)

This policy explains how the Android app Motorway Info (package com.zeljkobilbija001.motorwayinfo), developed by Željko Bilbija, handles your data.

Data we collect

The app has no user accounts. The developer does not collect, store or sell any personal data. The motorway information (exits, interchanges, service areas, fuel stations and rest areas) is built into the app and works offline.

Location (Driving mode and My position)

Only if you start Driving mode or tap My position, the app uses your device's location (precise and approximate) to find the motorway you are on, the mile and the direction of travel. Driving mode runs only after you start it with a button, shows a permanent notification while it is active and stops when you tap Stop or close the app. Your location is processed only on your device. It is not stored, not sent to the developer and not shared with anyone.

Traffic news and notifications

The app shows public traffic news (closures, roadworks, incidents) from official sources: National Highways (England), Traffic Wales (Wales) and the DfI Traffic Information and Control Centre (Northern Ireland). News for England is downloaded through a small relay server operated by the developer on Cloudflare Workers (evis-posrednik.zeljkobilbija.workers.dev), which only forwards the public data; news for Wales and Northern Ireland is downloaded directly from their public RSS feeds. Each request contains only the motorway name (for example “M25”) and no personal data and no location. As with any internet request, the server providers technically receive your IP address; the developer does not log or store it. If you allow notifications, the app checks the news about every 15 minutes in the background for the motorway you selected or are driving on. You can turn notifications off in the app at any time.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows ads from Google AdMob, a service of Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC. To serve and measure ads, Google may process device information such as the advertising ID, IP address, device model and app interactions, and may use cookies or similar technologies. Google may also process diagnostic data and, as part of its SDKs, other identifiers described in the Google policies linked below.

In the UK, the EU/EEA and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent through Google's consent form (User Messaging Platform) before ads are loaded. You can change your choice at any time with the Privacy button (shield) on the app's start screen. If you do not consent, Google may show non-personalised or limited ads.

More information: How Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings (Google › Ads).

The app itself never asks for, reads or stores your email address. The Google libraries built into the app (Google Mobile Ads, Google Play services and Google Play Billing) may process limited account-related information, such as an email address linked to your Google account, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, under Google's own privacy policy.

In-app purchase “Remove ads”

The optional one-time purchase is handled by Google Play Billing. Payment details are processed only by Google; the app only receives confirmation that the purchase exists, so that ads can be removed. See the Google Privacy Policy.

Sharing and maps

When you share an entry, open it in a maps app, call a phone number or open a website, the app hands the data to the app you choose. That app's own privacy policy then applies. Nothing is sent unless you tap one of these actions.

Data sources

Motorway data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL). Traffic news: Powered by National Highways’ Transport Data Feeds; Traffic Wales; DfI Traffic Information and Control Centre. Brand logos: Wikimedia Commons; trademarks belong to their respective owners.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Your rights

Under the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR you have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and data portability, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (in the UK: the Information Commissioner's Office, ico.org.uk). Because the developer does not hold any of your data, requests about advertising data are best directed to Google; you are also welcome to contact us.
